Urgench International Airport
(
IATA
:
UGC
,
ICAO
:
UZNU
)
is an international airport in
Urgench
,
Uzbekistan
. The airport is located 3 km from the center of Urgench and 30 km from
Khiva
.
[
3
]
Facilities
[
edit
]
Urgench International Airport was founded in 1932, and in 1975 its colonnaded terminal building was constructed
[
4
]
.
Domestic flight terminal
In 2006, a new terminal building was constructed with a capacity of 300 passengers per hour.
[
5
]
The airport operates daily flights to
Tashkent
, along with regular services to
Moscow
,
Saint Petersburg
, and
Mineralnye Vody
. Charter flights to various domestic and international destinations are also operated on demand for travel agencies. In September 2008, a trial flight from
Paris
to Urgench was conducted using a
Boeing 757
.
[
6
]
In 2014, the airport underwent a major two-stage modernization of its passenger infrastructure as part of a regional program to boost tourism in the Khorezm Region
[
7
]
[
8
]
. On May 22, 2014, a new arrival terminal was launched within the fully reconstructed 1975 building, designed by Uzshaharsozlik LITI and rebuilt by JSC 93-Mahsustrest
[
7
]
. This was followed on December 15, 2014, by the opening of a separate departure terminal, with each facility capable of handling over 300 passengers per hour
[
7
]
[
8
]
. The expanded complex features a 200-passenger transit lounge, dedicated VIP and CIP halls, a mother-and-child room, a medical station, duty-free shops, cafeterias, and a tourist information desk providing hotel and transport guidance
[
7
]
[
8
]
. The forecourt was also redeveloped with parking areas for cars and tour buses to support growing passenger volume, which reached 143,370 people in 2013, including nearly 46,000 foreign tourists
[
7
]
.
Runway and airfield
[
edit
]
The airport's single runway has a
PCN
of 59/F/B/X/T, accommodating aircraft up to a maximum takeoff weight of 310 tonnes. It is equipped to handle aircraft such as the
An-12
,
An-24
,
An-26
,
An-28
,
An-32
,
An-72
,
An-74
,
An-124
(on request),
Il-62
,
Il-76
,
Il-86
,
Il-96
,
L-410
,
Tu-134
,
Tu-154
,
Tu-204
,
Tu-214
,
Yak-40
,
Yak-42
,
Airbus A310
,
Airbus A319
,
Airbus A320
,
Airbus A321
,
ATR 42
,
ATR 72
,
Boeing 737
,
Boeing 747
,
Boeing 757
,
Boeing 767
,
Bombardier CRJ 100/200
,
Embraer EMB 120 Brasilia
,
Sukhoi Superjet 100
, and all types of helicopters.
[
9
]
In October 2026, a comprehensive airfield reconstruction project was initiated, including extending the runway from 3,000 meters to 3,300 meters and resurfacing it with concrete. The taxiway layout is being upgraded from a single taxiway to four, including a high-speed exit taxiway, while aircraft parking stands are being expanded from 6 to 18. These improvements will upgrade the airport's reference code from Class E to Class F, enabling it to accommodate large aircraft such as the
Boeing 747
-8 and
Airbus A380
. Additionally, the lighting system, originally installed in 1997 and operating well past its 10-year design life, is being replaced with modern ICAO-compliant equipment to upgrade the airfield's approach lighting standard from Category I to Category II. This upgrade will allow aircraft to land in visibility as low as 300 meters, down from the previous 750-meter threshold, addressing frequent winter fog conditions that historically disrupted flight operations
[
7
]
Reconstruction and Expansion
[
edit
]
In April 2025, South Korea's
Incheon International Airport Corporation
won an international tender for the comprehensive modernization and management of Urgench International Airport. The project is implemented as a
public–private partnership
(PPP) under a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) model covering a total period of 22 years, including a 3-year construction phase.
[
3
]
The project structure was finalized in December 2025 with the signing of the PPP agreement between
Uzbekistan Airports
, Urgench Airport, and the special purpose vehicle
ICN-Urgench Airport Services
. Total project investment is estimated at
US$
242 million, split between US$134 million from the private partner and US$108 million in state-funded infrastructure improvements.
[
3
]
Passenger Terminal:
Construction of a new 39,200 m² passenger terminal began in late 2026. Designed by Kirin Design and built by BoMi Engineering & Construction with Samsung providing technical supervision, the terminal is designed for a peak capacity of 1,000 passengers per hour (approx. 3 million annually).
[
3
]
Temporary Facilities:
Following the demolition of the original 1970s terminal building, a temporary arrival terminal was erected to maintain uninterrupted flight operations during construction.
[
3
]
Airside and Auxiliary Infrastructure:
The scope includes a new cargo terminal, catering facilities, and state-funded reconstruction of the runway, taxiways, and apron areas.
[
3
]
Construction is scheduled for completion by the end of 2028. Starting in 2029, Incheon International Airport Corporation will assume operational management of the passenger terminal for a 19-year concession period.
[
3
]

Ground transportation
[
edit
]
In September 2026,
Uzbekistan Railways
launched an electric train service directly connecting Urgench International Airport with the historic city of Khiva via Urgench railway station
[
28
]
. Constructed at a cost of 225 billion UZS, the project marked the first instance in Uzbekistan of a direct railway line integrated with an airport
[
28
]
. The 42-minute rail journey serves both domestic travelers and international tourists visiting Khiva's UNESCO World Heritage sites
[
28
]
.
