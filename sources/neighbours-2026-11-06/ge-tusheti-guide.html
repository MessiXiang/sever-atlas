Tusheti, Georgia: 2026 Guide to Omalo, Dartlo and Hikes

Tusheti is Georgia's most cut-off mountain region: 48 villages of slate towers in the far north-east, reached by one dirt road that is open for about four months a year. Snow shut the pass on 4 October 2026, the Roads Department let 4x4s back on 8 October, and snow closed it again on 24 October, so the 2026 season is over unless the department reopens it. This guide is about being there: which villages to base yourself in, the walks worth doing, the customs visitors are expected to follow, what the guesthouses cost and how the season works, so you can plan the 2027 season. Getting over the Abano Pass has its own guide. Everything checked in October 2026.

By The Georgian Guide team 16 min read Published February 2026 Updated October 2026

Quick answer

Give Tusheti at least three nights, between late June and the end of September. Get to Kvemo Alvani in Kakheti by the 09:10 marshrutka from Tbilisi (15 GEL), then take a seat in a shared 4x4 over the Abano Pass to Omalo for 100 to 120 GEL (about $38 to $46), 3.5 to 4 hours; your guesthouse will arrange it. Base yourself in Omalo for Keselo fortress and the walk to Shenako and Diklo, and spend a night in Dartlo, the best-kept tower village. Guesthouse rooms for two in Omalo were 80 to 120 GEL on Booking.com for a July 2027 Saturday, meals extra. There are no ATMs, no card machines and no hospital. The road opened on 20 June in 2026; snow closed the pass on 4 October, 4x4s were let back on 8 October, and since 24 October it has been closed to all vehicles again, so the next chance is late May or June 2027. Book with free cancellation and keep a spare day whenever you go late in the season.

Warning

Road status: closed to all vehicles since 24 October 2026

The Roads Department of Georgia prohibited all traffic on km 22 to 72 of the Pshaveli, Abano, Omalo road, which is the pass, at 14:16 on 4 October 2026 for snow and black ice. At 14:28 on 8 October it let high-clearance vehicles back on km 12 to 72, Lechuri to Omalo, and at 13:56 on 24 October it prohibited all vehicles on km 22 to 72 again for snow and icy road, leaving km 1 to 21 unrestricted. That was still the newest entry when we read the list at 03:53 on 28 October. The same thing happened in 2025: closed on 13 October, 4x4s back on 22 October, shut for the winter on 6 November. So plan as if the 2026 season is over unless a Traffic Restored entry appears. Check the newest entry on georoad.gov.ge/en/restriction, or our road status board, the day before you travel; our road guide explains how to read it.

What Tusheti is

Tusheti is a historic region in the Greater Caucasus in Georgia's far north-east, around 200 km from Tbilisi, bordered by Chechnya and Dagestan to the north and east and by Pshavi and Khevsureti to the west. Administratively it is part of Kakheti; in every other way it is its own place. Since 2003 most of it has been the Tusheti Protected Areas, 113,660 hectares of national park, protected landscape and strict nature reserve rising from about 900 m to 4,493 m at Mount Tebulo. Three valleys make it up, and each of their rivers is called Alazani. The figures in this section come from wander-lush.org's Tusheti guide, updated 22 September 2026, and caucasus-trekking.com's region guide.

Tusheti once had about 5,000 permanent residents. Soviet policy moved families down to the Kakheti lowlands in the 1940s and 1950s, the road up was finished around 1980, and today most Tush families spend summer in the mountains and winter in Alvani, the lowland village where the jeeps leave from. So Tusheti is lived in from about June to October. Omalo, the main village, sits at 1,880 m; Bochorna, at 2,345 m, is often called the highest continuously inhabited village in Europe and has one full-time resident. People speak a Tush dialect of Georgian, and the Tsova Tush speak Bats, an endangered language of their own. Most do not follow Orthodox Christianity so much as their own shrines, the khati, which matters for how you behave (see below). Tourism has grown from under 1,000 visitors in 2008 to more than 15,000 a year, by caucasus-trekking's count, which is still a small number spread over a short season.

How high is Tusheti?

High enough that nights are cold in July and the pass can see snow in any month, but Omalo itself sits lower than most people expect: at 1,880 m it is a little above Stepantsminda (1,740 m) and below Ushguli (2,100 m). The crossing of the Abano Pass is the only time most visitors spend above 2,500 m, and it lasts an hour or two in a car. The figures below are from wander-lush and caucasus-trekking; the pass has no single agreed height.

PlaceAltitude and what it means
Omalo1,880 m. Where the road ends and most people sleep; cold nights, mild days in summer
Bochorna2,345 m. The highest inhabited village in Europe by the usual claim, with one permanent resident

Getting there

One road leads in: from Kvemo Alvani in Kakheti over the Abano Pass to Omalo, about 77 km at 20 to 30 km/h. Rental cars are banned from it by Localrent and almost every other agency. The usual way is a marshrutka to Kvemo Alvani (from Ortachala at 09:10, 15 GEL, about 2.5 hours; take the first one) and then a seat in a six-seat Delica that leaves when full, 100 to 120 GEL a person one way, or 500 to 650 GEL for the whole jeep. Drivers gather at the main junction in Kvemo Alvani from about 11:00 and the last leave by early afternoon, so be there by 11:30. The fares are the 2026 season's from wander-lush's Tusheti transport guide updated 25 September 2026 and match our road to Tusheti guide, which has the whole drive, the safety rules and the jeep tours.

The door-to-door alternative is a GoTrip driver from Tbilisi: one listing on gotrip.ge on 14 October 2026, a six-seat Toyota Fortuner at 755 GEL (about $291) for the car, down from 1,136 GEL on 6 October, 185 km, with a quoted drive time of 4 h 15 min that the pass will stretch to closer to six. The cheaper hybrid is a GoTrip sedan from Tbilisi to Kvemo Alvani, 191 to 202 GEL (about $73 to $78) per car on the same day, and a shared jeep from there. Whichever you choose, book the guesthouse first and ask the host to arrange the driver, and never travel with one who has been drinking. Draw cash in Kvemo Alvani: its ATM is the last one.

Warning

The season is short and ends without much notice

The Roads Department opens the pass when the snow is cleared: 20 June in 2026, a late year after a winter that left snow more than 15 m deep on the avalanche sections, by the department's April statement. It normally closes for the winter in the second half of October or early November; in 2026 snow closed it on 4 October, the department let 4x4s back on 8 October and snow closed it again on 24 October. Snow, heavy rain or landslides can shut it for days at any point: in July 2026 a flood washed out the approach to the Shenako bridge and cut Shenako and Diklo off from Omalo for a day until the protected areas administration had it repaired. Families and flocks start going down in late September. If you go after mid September, leave a spare day at the end and confirm the road with your guesthouse the day before you travel.

If you would rather hand the logistics to someone else, three-day jeep tours run from Telavi and Tbilisi through the season with transport, a guide and guesthouse nights included. We have not taken one, so compare the number of nights in Tusheti (two minimum), which villages beyond Omalo are included and what happens to your money if the pass closes.

The villages

Omalo. Where the road ends, most people sleep and the police station sits. Lower Omalo is the larger part, with the Tusheti Protected Areas visitor centre (a small museum, trail maps for about 7 GEL, English-speaking staff and a phone line, +995 577 10 18 92), a few small shops and most of the guesthouses. Upper Omalo is smaller and prettier, on the hill under Keselo, the fortress of restored slate towers that is the first thing to walk to, ideally at sunset; the Keselo foundation rebuilt several of the towers from 2003. Upper Omalo also has the two places that pass for restaurants, both bars with some food. Women should keep to the paths at Keselo: there is a sacred hill beside the fortress, and the Tsasne forest at the edge of Upper Omalo is closed to women.

Dartlo. The village to spend a night in if you only add one. It sits in the Pirikiti valley about 15 km from Omalo, an hour on a rough track or 10.9 km on foot by the trail, and is a 17th-century fortified village of reconstructed towers and stone houses with a sabcheo, the open-air court where elders met on stone benches, plus petroglyphs and church ruins. Several family guesthouses sit inside or at the edge of the old village, and Samtsikhe has a restaurant. The short trek of about 7 km from Dartlo up to the tower hamlets of Kvavlo and Dano was wander-lush's favourite experience in Tusheti.

Shenako. A small, pastoral village a short drive or a couple of hours on foot from Omalo, with wooden balconied houses and St George's church, consecrated in 1843 and still in use. It is the only village on an electricity grid, thanks to a hydropower plant, and a good lunch stop on the way to Diklo. Its road bridge is the one the July 2026 flood took out; it was rebuilt within a day.

Diklo. The last village before the Dagestan border, a few hundred metres from it, with alpine views and the ruin of Diklo fortress. Since 2022 visiting Diklo fortress and Lower Diklo needs a border permit, and border officers do check; ask at the visitor centre before you walk out, and carry your passport everywhere in Tusheti. One of the handful of people who winter in Diklo keeps a small shop of hand-knitted socks and hats.

Beyond these four, the Pirikiti valley runs on past Dartlo to Parsma and Girevi, smaller villages reached on foot, on horseback or on the Omalo to Shatili trek, which passes through them. Tsaro and Chontio have tower complexes too. Towers across Tusheti were mostly built in the 16th and 17th centuries against raids from the north, dry-stacked from local slate with pyramid roofs, and most are damaged; Dartlo and Keselo are where you see them restored.

Tusheti National Park and the visitor centre

The Tusheti Protected Areas, run by the Agency of Protected Areas since 2003, cover almost the whole region in three tiers: the national park, the protected landscape that takes in the villages, and a strict nature reserve. There is no entry fee and no registration, by wander-lush's September 2026 guide, though caucasus-trekking reports the administration is considering one to pay for the strain tourism puts on water and waste. A voluntary donation scheme from 2018 suggested 10 to 20 GEL, which would be 20 to 30 GEL today; the box is at the visitor centre in Lower Omalo, which also sells the trail map for about 7 GEL and souvenirs. The centre has a small museum on the region's wildlife and a few rooms of its own, its staff speak English, and the administration's number, +995 577 101 892, is the one to call about trail and pass conditions before you set out. Some routes near the border need a permit: Diklo fortress and Lower Diklo since 2022, and the border post at Girevi registers walkers heading for the Atsunta pass.

Walks and treks

Walking between villages is the point of Tusheti. The visitor centre's map covers the 13 main marked trails, which use Georgia's usual yellow signs and red and white paint marks; caucasus-trekking.com has detailed descriptions and GPS tracks. Distances below are from its route pages.

RouteDistance, time and notes
Keselo fortressUp from Lower Omalo to the towers above Upper Omalo. Short and steep. Go at sunset
Omalo to Shenako and DikloAbout 11 km one way to Diklo fortress, around 4 hours, 660 m of climbing, easy; a full day if you walk back. Border permit needed for the fortress
Omalo to Dartlo10.9 km, about 405 m up and 670 m down, mostly on the jeep road with a short cut; day one of the Shatili trek, and an easy first walk
Dartlo to Kvavlo and DanoA short trek of about 7 km from Dartlo to the tower hamlets above it
Oreti lake28.5 km there and back from Lower Omalo, about 1,680 m of climbing, 9 hours. Hard
Omalo to Jvarboseli24 km over two days via Gogrulta, 1,770 m of climbing, moderate; guesthouse at each end
Omalo to Shatili75 km in 4 to 5 days over the Atsunta pass at 3,431 m into Khevsureti, the classic trek: Dartlo, Girevi (14 km), Kvakhidi meadows (14.2 km), Khidotani ridge (16.8 km), Shatili. Hiking shelters at Kvakhidi and Khidotani since 2024, unlocked but unstaffed in 2026 and sometimes found locked, so carry a tent. Border guards decide when the pass opens: July in 2025 and 2026, and not before 10 July in 2026 after the heavy snow. Best in July and August

Our Shatili guide covers the far end of the trek. For the Atsunta route and any multi-day walk, go with a guide or at least with GPS tracks and someone at the visitor centre who knows your plan; there is no rescue close by. Wild camping is allowed, but around Omalo and Dartlo stick to marked campsites or a guesthouse garden, keep away from shrines, and only light fires at marked campsites. Shepherd dogs guarding flocks are the one real hazard on the trails: give flocks a wide berth and ask locally about problem spots. Horses are the other way to cover ground: about a dozen Omalo families hire them out at 40 to 50 GEL a day per horse plus a horseman, by caucasus-trekking; on the Shatili trek a horse to carry the bags is about 200 GEL a day with its handler, plus the two days it takes him to get home.

Customs to respect

Tusheti's rules are real, not folklore for visitors, and your hosts will notice. These are from wander-lush's September 2026 guide, which was written with an interpreter and local hosts.

  • Keep away from khati. The shrines, small stone houses with a door opening, often topped with horns or marked with flags, are for men or for women; women should not approach men's khati, church ruins or other marked sacred places at all, and some paths are closed to women. Ask your host what to avoid before you walk out.
  • No pork. The mountains are considered pure and pork is taboo up here. Do not bring any, jerky included.
  • Dismount in villages. Riding through a village without stopping once meant an attack. Lead the horse through.
  • Dress modestly. Long, loose trousers and covered shoulders, for men and women.
  • Drink at your own pace. Toasts and aludi, the Tush beer, come with dinner, and some hosts press hard. It is fine to decline politely.

When to go

PeriodWhat to expect
JuneThe road opens some time in the month, 20 June in 2026. Waterfalls at their fullest, wildflowers, rain and chill; high rivers close some back roads and not every guesthouse is open. Flocks come up from the lowlands
July and AugustHigh season and the best walking weather: low 30s by day in Omalo, cold nights. Busiest roads and guesthouses. Atnigenoba, the biggest Tush festival, moves from village to village through August, and Peristsvaleba is on 19 August
SeptemberOur pick for walking between villages: dry, quiet, autumn colour. Daytime temperatures fall to the low teens by the end of the month, nights to single figures, and the shepherds start down
OctoberFirst snow up high early in the month, guesthouses closing, families leaving. The road has stayed open into the second half of the month in recent years; in 2026 snow closed it on 4 October, 4x4s were let back on 8 October and snow closed it again on 24 October, so go only with a spare day and a guesthouse that confirms it is open. The Tushetian cheese festival is in Alvani, down in Kakheti, at the start of the month
November to MayClosed by snow. Only a handful of people stay the winter

Atnigenoba falls 100 days after Orthodox Easter, so its date shifts every year. Zezvaoba, with its 4 km horse race ending in Kvemo Alvani, is on the last Sunday of May in Alvani, before the road opens. Our best time to visit guide fits Tusheti into the rest of the country, and the October guide explains why Tusheti is the one region to leave out of an autumn trip.

Where to stay

Family guesthouses, with a couple of hotels in Upper Omalo. Expect a simple room, shared or private bathroom, lukewarm solar hot water, no heating (there are thick blankets) and the best food of the trip at the family table. Electricity comes from solar panels and generators everywhere except Shenako, so sockets are few and in the common rooms, hair dryers are out, and on a cloudy day there may be no power at all; bring a power bank and a multi-port charger. Wander-lush's rule of thumb is 80 to 120 GEL a person a night with breakfast, dinner 20 to 30 GEL more; the room-only prices on Booking.com below are lower. More of Tusheti is on Booking.com every year, which is how we book it: free cancellation matters when the road can close. Many smaller places still book by phone or Facebook, and tour companies reserve the bigger guesthouses a year ahead, so book July and August early. Pay in cash.

Food and drink

Meals come from your guesthouse, and in most villages there is nowhere else: two bar-cafes in Upper Omalo and a restaurant in Dartlo serve drinks and some food, and the shops are small, not always open and dear. Order dinner a day ahead if you are not staying where you eat. It is a shepherds' table: sheep's cheese, above all guda, meat, dough and whatever the garden gives, and never pork. Aludi is the Tush beer, brewed from mountain barley and hops, lightly fizzy and with very little alcohol, once made only for festivals and now poured all summer at some guesthouses; a glass, or a cup of Tushetian tea, is 3 to 5 GEL where it is sold. Tush wool is the souvenir: felt and hand-knitted socks and hats at 50 to 100 GEL, sold at many guesthouses and by the few people who winter in Diklo.

Practical details

Phone and internet. Better than its reputation. Wander-lush's writers had working 4G in Omalo, Dartlo, Shenako and Diklo in 2026, patchy signal only on the drive up and none in Jvarboseli, and Wi-Fi at every guesthouse. Magti is the network to buy and Beeline does not cover the region; our SIM card guide compares them. Download offline maps and GPS tracks anyway before you leave Alvani.

Health and insurance. There is no hospital in Tusheti and one licensed doctor, and serious injuries mean a helicopter. Georgia has required every foreign visitor to hold medical cover since 1 January 2026; for Tusheti make sure it includes evacuation and hiking. Our insurance guide explains the rule.

Border zone. Carry your passport at all times, including on walks; an EU identity card may not satisfy the border police. The helicopters you hear are usually the border police changing shift.

Packing. Warm sleeping layers for unheated rooms, waterproofs, proper boots for walking, sandals for the guesthouse, quick-dry clothes (nowhere has a dryer), sun cream and insect repellent. The packing list generator builds a list for your month.

Mistakes to avoid

  • One night. Six hours up and six down for one evening. Three nights, or two at the very least.
  • Not enough cash. No ATMs, no cards. The last machine is in Kvemo Alvani.
  • A rental car. The contract bans the road and the insurance goes with it.
  • Missing the morning jeeps. Take the 09:10 marshrutka; later ones reach Alvani after the drivers have gone.
  • Ignoring the shrines. Ask where you may walk before you go exploring, especially as a woman.
  • Counting on October. Snow closed the pass for four days from 4 October in 2026. September is the latest month to count on; an October trip needs a spare day and a refundable room.
  • Walking towards Diklo fortress without a permit. Border officers turn people back.

Frequently asked questions

Is the road to Tusheti open now?
No. Snow and black ice closed the pass section of the Pshaveli, Abano, Omalo road on 4 October 2026, the Roads Department let high-clearance vehicles back on 8 October, and at 13:56 on 24 October it prohibited all vehicles on km 22 to 72 again for snow and icy road; that was the newest entry on 28 October. In 2025 the second closure, on 6 November, lasted the winter. The department lists every closure and reopening at georoad.gov.ge/en/restriction.
How do you get to Tusheti?
Take the 09:10 marshrutka from Ortachala in Tbilisi to Kvemo Alvani (15 GEL, about 2.5 hours), then a seat in a shared 4x4 over the Abano Pass to Omalo for 100 to 120 GEL, 3.5 to 4 hours; your guesthouse will arrange the driver. A whole jeep from Alvani is 500 to 650 GEL, and a GoTrip driver from Tbilisi was 755 GEL per car on 14 October 2026. Rental cars are not allowed on the road.
When is the road to Tusheti open?
From when the snow is cleared, 20 June in 2026, until it closes for winter, usually the second half of October or early November. In 2026 snow closed it on 4 October, 4x4s were let back on 8 October and snow closed it again on 24 October. Snow, rain and landslides can close it for days at any time, so leave a spare day if you go late in the season.
How many days do you need in Tusheti?
Three nights at least: a day to recover from the drive and see Omalo and Keselo, a day to walk to Shenako and Diklo, and a night in Dartlo. Four or five if you want the longer walks, and a week for the Omalo to Shatili trek.
Is Tusheti a national park?
Most of it. The Tusheti Protected Areas, created in 2003, cover 113,660 hectares as a national park, a protected landscape that includes the villages, and a strict nature reserve. Entry is free and there is no registration; a donation of 20 to 30 GEL is suggested at the visitor centre in Lower Omalo, and Diklo fortress and the border routes need a permit.
How high is Tusheti?
Omalo, where the road ends, is at 1,880 m, and Bochorna at 2,345 m is called the highest inhabited village in Europe. The Abano Pass on the way in is about 2,826 m by most guides (the Roads Department says 2,950 m), the Atsunta trekking pass 3,431 m, and Mount Tebulo, the highest point, 4,493 m.
Tusheti or Svaneti?
Svaneti if you want easy access, more comfort and the famous towers of Mestia and Ushguli; it is reachable all year on sealed roads. Tusheti if you want real remoteness, walks between villages with few other people and a culture that feels apart from the rest of Georgia; it takes more effort and has a four-month season. Both have excellent guesthouses; Svaneti has ATMs in Mestia, Tusheti has none.
Are the people of Tusheti Muslim?
No. Tush families are Georgian and the region has Orthodox churches, including St George in Shenako, consecrated in 1843 and still in use, but most people worship at the khati shrines of their own older belief system rather than in church. Centuries of trade and conflict with the North Caucasus left Islamic and animist influences in the customs, which is why pork is taboo in the mountains.
Is there phone signal in Tusheti?
Yes, in most villages. In 2026 there was working 4G in Omalo, Dartlo, Shenako and Diklo, and guesthouses have Wi-Fi. Signal is patchy on the road up and on remote trails. Magti has the best coverage; Beeline does not cover Tusheti.
Is Tusheti safe?
For crime, yes. The risks are the road (travel only with a sober, experienced driver and not after heavy rain), the weather, shepherd dogs on the trails and the distance from medical help: there is no hospital, so carry insurance that covers evacuation. Carry your passport, because Tusheti is a border zone.
